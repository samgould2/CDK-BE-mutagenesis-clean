# Evolutionary conservation of the kinase pocket

Generated by `notebooks/07_KLIFS/conservation_analysis.ipynb` and the scripts alongside it.

Two independent axes of constraint are measured:

| Axis | Question it answers | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Kinome-wide** | At this pocket position, do different human kinases use different residues? | KLIFS 85-residue pocket alignment, all human kinases |
| **Cross-species** | Has evolution ever changed this residue *within this CDK*? | deep homolog search across all domains of life + MAFFT |

A residue fixed on **both** axes is catalytic core and cannot be mutated. A residue fixed
across species but variable across the kinome is a **CDK-specific functional site** — the
class that matters for inhibitor selectivity and for interpreting the base-editing screen.

## Method notes

**Conservation** is `1 − H / log₂20`, where `H` is Shannon entropy over the 20 amino acids
with gaps excluded. 1 = invariant, 0 = uniformly random.

**Size-matched null.** A small, closely related group of kinases will always look more
conserved than the whole kinome simply because it is small. For each group we therefore draw
1000 random size-matched subsets of the kinome and report the excess over that null
(`excess_conservation`, `z_conservation`), plus the KL divergence of the group's residue
preference from the kinome's (`KL_vs_kinome`, `z_KL`) — this last one flags
specificity-determining positions, where the group prefers a *different* residue.

**Clear homolog.** A single BLAST against all of UniProtKB saturates its 1000-hit cap inside
the vertebrates and never reaches fungi, plants or protists, so the search is run separately
per taxonomic division. Hits are kept when identity ≥ 25%, E ≤ 1e-15, subject length ≥ 200,
and **bit score per human kinase-domain residue ≥ 1.3**. That last criterion is
length-independent and cleanly separates true CDK orthologs (2–3.5) from generic kinase-fold
proteins such as archaeal and bacterial Ser/Thr kinases (< 1), which are recorded in
`*_rejected_distant_hits.csv` rather than silently dropped. One representative per organism
is kept, clades are capped at 220 sequences so mammals do not swamp the alignment, and
sequences covering < 60% of the Pfam kinase domain are removed after alignment.

**Sequence weighting.** Cross-species conservation uses Henikoff & Henikoff position-based
weights, so the many near-identical mammalian sequences do not dominate the score.

## Screen integration

`TABLE_conservation_vs_screen.xlsx` (pooled) and
`TABLE_conservation_vs_screen_per_CDK.xlsx` (primary, per CDK), built by
`screen_conservation_integration.py`, `screen_conservation_stats.py` and
`screen_conservation_per_cdk.py`.

Edit identity comes from the **canonical** HGVSp tables and follows the thresholds in
`09_analysis_and_viz/fig1_analysis.ipynb`: ≥10% editing, ≥100 sensor reads, top non-WT edit
per guide. Only unambiguous single substitutions are used, so a residue can be attributed;
the edited reference residue matches the UniProt sequence for 100% of mapped guides.

Outcomes are per guide, not per table row — the master LFC tables are stacked across
Screen_ID and reference block, so a guide appears ~8 times and its drug and fitness contrasts
sit on different rows. A guide is a hit if any of its rows clears the threshold.

* **resistance** — FDR < 0.01 and LFC > 1 in any inhibitor that targets that CDK
* **fitness** — FDR < 0.01 and LFC < −1 in T0 vs plasmid or DMSO vs T0

**Nonsense edits are excluded from the primary test** and kept as a positive control: they
deplete because they truncate the protein, not because the edited residue is conserved.
The control behaves as it should — nonsense in the first 70% of the CDS depletes (3.1% hit
rate, median LFC −1.09) while nonsense in the last 30% does not (0%, −0.77).

**gRNA editing efficiency** is both a filter and a covariate in every model, and the
hit-rate curves are stratified by editing-efficiency tertile.

Estimates are made **within each CDK** and pooled with a DerSimonian-Laird random-effects
meta-analysis. CDK2, CDK4 and CDK6 yield too few fitness hits (0–3) to model.

### Confidence tiers

`TABLE_conservation_confidence_tiers.xlsx` (`screen_conservation_confidence.py`) repeats the
per-CDK test under three hit definitions:

| | stats | editing | corroboration |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 standard | FDR < 0.01, \|LFC\| > 1 | ≥10% | — |
| T2 high | FDR < 0.001, \|LFC\| > 2 | ≥50% | — |
| T3 replicated | FDR < 0.01, \|LFC\| > 1 | ≥10% | same variant called by ≥2 independent gRNAs |

The editing floor applies to the whole population, not just the hits — filtering only hits on
a variable that itself tracks outcome would manufacture the association. Guides clearing the
standard threshold but not the tier threshold are dropped, so the comparison group is never
contaminated with real-but-weak hits.

T3 uses independent-gRNA replication rather than agreement between the two fitness contrasts,
because no guide is significant in both T0-vs-plasmid and DMSO-vs-T0 — those capture
different dropout windows (library production vs the screen itself).

Sheet `Highest_confidence_variants` is the replicated variant list (43 fitness, 8 resistance).
